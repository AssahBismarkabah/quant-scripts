# S04 Policy Closure: Index-Mandated Spin-Off Sales No Longer Exist

**Date:** 2026-09-21
**Status:** FEASIBILITY RECORD - primary-source closure of the S04 subtype.

## The question

S04 asked whether a modern event exists where index-tracking funds are
mandated to sell a spun-off company at its ex-date (the classic forced
supply event). The 2026-09-17 registry checkpoint found the mechanism
supported in principle by S&P methodology, but no qualifying modern event.

## Primary evidence

1. S&P DJI policy change, September 2017
   ([PRNewswire 300142672](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sp-dow-jones-indices-announces-change-in-treatment-of-spin-offs-in-us-indices-300142672.html)):
   after a client consultation, S&P DJI standardized zero-price spin-off
   treatment for ALL indices, including the S&P 1500 and headline U.S.
   indices. A spin-off is added to every index containing the parent,
   weighted at zero price, and removed at the end of its first trading
   day unless the index committee elects to retain it (retention announced
   in advance per the corporate-actions methodology).

2. Practitioner confirmation (BofA Securities 424B2 prospectus text,
   SEC Archives): the spin-off is added to the S&P 500 on the ex-date at
   zero price, remains for at least one trading day, and there is no
   index-divisor change on the ex-date.

3. Modern outcome examples, all consistent with the policy:
   - SNDK (Western Digital, Feb 2025): ADDED to S&P SmallCap 600 while
     the parent stayed in the S&P 500 - addition, not exclusion.
   - SOLS (Honeywell, Oct-Dec 2025): added to the S&P 500 on the spin
     date (Oct 30, 2025), then deleted from the S&P 500 and ADDED to the
     S&P SmallCap 600 in the same announced quarterly rebalance
     (effective Dec 22, 2025; announced Dec 5, 2025). It never traded
     outside an S&P index.
   - Q (DuPont's Qnity, Nov 3, 2025): added to the S&P 500 at spin.
   - HONA (Honeywell Aerospace, Jun 2026): welcomed into the S&P 500.

## Conclusion

The S04 event type - ex-date index-mandated dumping of a spinco - was
eliminated by the 2017 zero-price policy: index funds receive the spinco
inside the index at zero cost and dispose of it later, through announced,
scheduled rebalances. Those migration trades belong to the project's
already-closed index-rebalancing family. No qualifying modern S04 event
can exist; the search is closed on policy evidence, not just on failure
to find an example.

## Broader B15 implication

With S04 closed by policy, no reviewed primary document from the 2024-2026
census supplied evidence of a defined recipient selling constraint, and
the Abarbanell-Bushee-Raedy (2006) update of the 1993 study finds
institutional rebalancing after spin-offs is generally NOT associated
with abnormal price movements, the B15 spin-off family terminates for
this project cycle as UNVERIFIABLE - NO CONSTRAINT EVIDENCE. This is a
current-capability finding: a future regime change (a new index policy or
a constraint-bearing document class not yet reviewed) would be a legitimate
reopen condition requiring a re-frozen census.
