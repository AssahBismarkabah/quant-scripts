# Spin-Off Primary Review Notes

This is a source-review checkpoint, not a return study. The examples below show why a filing can confirm transaction mechanics without confirming the proposed supply mechanism.

| Candidate | Primary evidence | What it establishes | What remains unproven |
|---|---|---|---|
| Western Digital / SanDisk | [SEC 8-K, 2025-01-31](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/106040/000119312525019294/d922460d8k.htm) | 80.1% pro-rata distribution; record date 2025-02-12; distribution effective 2025-02-21; regular-way SNDK trading expected 2025-02-24 | No evidence that recipients were required to sell, nor that supply exceeded demand |
| Lennar / Millrose | [SEC 8-K, 2025-01-21](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/920760/000119312525009205/d913968d8k.htm) | Taxable spin-off and commencement of the shareholder election period were announced | This filing does not establish the final distribution terms, recipient selling, or an executable price discrepancy |
| Honeywell / Advanced Materials | [SEC exhibit, 2025-02-06](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/773840/000077384025000005/exhibit991-q42024earningsr.htm) | A planned separation was announced, with intended completion in H2 2026 | It was not yet a completed/tradable event in this document; no supply or timing mechanism is established |

## Strongest example: Western Digital / SanDisk

The governing information statement confirms a pro-rata distribution, a fixed record date, a distribution date, and the expected when-issued and regular-way trading sequence. It also says holders take no action and pay no consideration to receive the shares. [SEC information statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/106040/000119312525019294/d922460dex991.htm)

That is strong evidence of an observable corporate event, but it cuts against the original forced-supply hypothesis: the document does not say recipients are required to sell. It describes holders as receiving the security automatically and explicitly frames the information statement as helping them evaluate whether to hold or dispose of it. Therefore this example currently passes event observability but fails the defined participant-constraint gate.

## Additional confirmed examples

- Lennar/Millrose was completed on 2025-02-07. The SEC filing specifies an approximately 80% distribution, the record date, the 8:30 a.m. ET completion time, the NYSE listing, and Lennar’s retained approximately 20% position. [SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/920760/000119312525022309/d933374d8k.htm) These are precise mechanics, but retained shares and shareholder elections do not prove forced recipient selling.
- MDU/Everus was completed on 2024-10-31 according to MDU’s official filing exhibit. [SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/67716/000006771625000007/a2024earningsreleaseex-99.htm) The later disclosure confirms completion retrospectively, but does not provide a pre-trade timestamp or a defined seller constraint for a prospective signal.

## Important mechanism evidence found

The seller hypothesis is not purely invented. A peer-reviewed study specifically examines institutional share-allocation sales after corporate spin-offs and reports that price pressure is related to characteristics proxying for institutional investment restrictions. [Institutional Demand and Security Price Pressure: The Case of Corporate Spinoffs](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/1993/institutional-demand-and-security-price-pressure-the-case-of-corporate-spinoffs)

Separately, S&P Dow Jones Indices documents that when a spun-off company is ineligible for the parent index, an index-tracking fund should sell the spun-off company at the close of the ex-date. [S&P spin-off treatment consultation](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/index-policies/20150205-spin-off-consultation.pdf)

This establishes that a real seller mechanism can exist in some spin-offs. It does not establish that every spin-off has it, that the signal is available early enough, or that the opportunity remains after costs. It also overlaps materially with the project’s already-closed index-rebalancing family. Any test of this specific index-mandated subtype would require an explicit governance amendment.

The newly supplied Abarbanell, Bushee, and Raedy paper materially qualifies the older positive claim. Its abstract says institutional styles and fiduciary restrictions affect holdings after spin-offs, but that this trading is generally **not associated with abnormal price movements** around the event. The paper’s sample section reports 169 transactions with required data from 1980–1996 after screening and verification. It therefore supports the existence of preference-driven rebalancing, but not a reliable tradeable price-pressure edge. [Journal of Business paper](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/367749)

The modern SanDisk example does not currently qualify as S04: S&P announced SanDisk as an addition to the S&P SmallCap 600 effective February 25, 2025, while Western Digital remained in the S&P 500. [S&P index announcement](https://press.spglobal.com/2025-02-19-SanDisk-Set-to-Join-S-P-SmallCap-600) This confirms that a real spin-off need not create index-mandated selling of the child.

An older but concrete near-match is TechnipFMC/Technip Energies. S&P announced TechnipFMC’s removal ahead of the spin-off and stated that Technip Energies was expected to trade as an OTC ADR. [S&P announcement](https://press.spglobal.com/2021-02-08-Monolithic-Power-Systems-Set-to-Join-S-P-500-Iridium-Communications-to-Join-S-P-MidCap-400-Collegium-Pharmaceutical-to-Join-S-P-SmallCap-600) This is evidence that a spin-off can create index and venue complications, but the OTC execution path and fund-disposal mechanics are not established for our current capability set.

## Checkpoint disposition

These examples pass the basic “corporate event exists” check, but none passes the participant-constraint gate. They remain review candidates only. No event is eligible for a trading test from these notes.
